Prediction market agent guide

Forecasting asks what is likely. Trading asks whether a quote is wrong.

A forecast can stop at a probability. A prediction market trading agent still has to price an executable order, survive liquidity and risk limits, and leave a record of why it acted.

Inspect the open-source decision loop

The useful distinction: forecasting estimates an outcome; trading decides whether a specific order is worth taking now.

Settlement Edge today: 0 live orders and 0 TST realized competition P&L.

The short answer

Probability is an input, not the finish line.

A forecasting agent researches a question and returns a probability or categorical answer. That output can be useful without any market access.

A trading agent must turn the estimate into a decision against a live price. It needs to know what one or more shares cost, how that order moves the price, how much capital is exposed, and whether the expected edge remains after those constraints.

The job, side by side

DecisionForecasting agentTrading agent
Primary outputA probability, forecast, or answerA buy, sell, hold, or no-trade decision
Market priceUseful as a baseline or comparisonRequired to judge whether an order has edge
LiquidityUsually outside the forecasting taskChanges cost, impact, and viable order size
RiskOften scored by forecast accuracy or calibrationAlso bounded by spend, exposure, slippage, and execution failure
TimingCan be valuable well before resolutionMust act while the quote is still mispriced
Audit trailShould preserve sources and reasoningShould also preserve the quote, size, policy gates, and transaction state

Where settlement evidence fits

Use narrow facts when the resolution rule is narrow.

Some markets resolve from a specific public observation: a reported tide level, a match result, a published index value, or another machine-readable fact. In those cases, the hard part may be precise extraction and timing rather than broad world-model research.

Settlement Edge is built for that narrow case. Each reviewed rule names a source, the value to extract, its timestamp requirements, and the outcome mapping. Invalid, stale, future-dated, or off-rule evidence fails closed.

This approach is a poor fit for questions that depend on ambiguous wording, disputed interpretation, or information that cannot be mapped to a stable primary source.

A trading decision earns its way through

Five gates after the fact arrives.

  1. 01
    Validate the evidence

    Reject the observation if its source, shape, time, or rule mapping is unsafe.

  2. 02
    Estimate conservatively

    Shrink confidence toward the market when signals disagree or weaken.

  3. 03
    Request executable quotes

    Test small candidate sizes on the actual market curve.

  4. 04
    Apply risk limits

    Reject spend, portfolio share, price impact, or slippage outside the caps.

  5. 05
    Record the result

    Hash-link the source, estimate, quote, gate result, and later lifecycle state.

What the replay proves

Mechanics are observed. Performance is not.

The checked-in replay runs the full evidence-to-decision loop without credentials. In that simulation, a four-share plan cost 2.5200 TST and showed 1.4292 TST expected P&L.

No order was submitted. Settlement Edge has not yet observed a live order, completed redemption, or realized competition profit. The project records realized P&L only after both submitted cost basis and completed redemption are present.
